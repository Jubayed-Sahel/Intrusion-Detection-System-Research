# Publication extension results — 8 November 2026

The research now tests how the conclusions change with the masking intervention, LIME configuration, and source partition. Follow-up protocols were written before their own runs, after the primary results were known. The original primary experiment is preserved.

- **Masking:** Using 800 matched records and three replacement rules, nearest training donors reduced GeNIS TreeSHAP-minus-LIME mean gaps to 0.0023–0.0109. Three of four GeNIS pointwise mean intervals included zero. The direction-frequency sign test measures a different quantity. See [replacement methods and results](MASKING_EXTENSION_RESULTS_20261108.md).
- **LIME sampling and neighborhood:** Two fresh LIME budgets, 350 and 3,500 draws, produced 1,600 explanations. Higher budget alone did not close the median/mode gap. A separate width grid added 1,600 narrower-width fits and replayed the 800 default-width fits on identical neighborhoods. The narrowest width improved mean query fit in all eight contexts, but did not consistently improve deletion. TreeSHAP had positive mean gaps in all 16 new width comparisons; 13 passed the separate corrected sign tests. See the [budget protocol](LIME_BUDGET_PROTOCOL_20261108.md), [width protocol](LIME_KERNEL_PROTOCOL_20261108.md), and their results under `outputs/`.
- **Source holdout:** 64 detector refits and 64 paired explanation contexts across 16 source folds. There is no train/test source or connection-group overlap within a fold. GeNIS scenario 6 binary XGBoost missed 84.46% of attacks; multiclass XGBoost missed all 3,853 held-out brute-force records despite training support. Gotham's combined-cycle-1 bundle has 14,242 records from two labels absent from training; those labels count in all-test macro-F1. Median/mode mean differences favor TreeSHAP in 62 of 64 refits and LIME in two. These are descriptive within-testbed results, not 64 independent replications. See [protocol](SOURCE_HOLDOUT_PROTOCOL_20261108.md), [predictions](outputs/source_holdout_20261108/predictive_summary.csv), and [explanations](outputs/source_holdout_20261108/explanation_summary.csv).

The source verification uses a 1e-12 absolute probability tolerance for matched random controls to accommodate floating-point roundoff and independently replays all group-mean differences. To reproduce the saved-output audit, run `python outputs/source_holdout_20261108/verify_saved_results.py` in the pinned environment. The complete experiment can be rerun from its frozen protocol and inputs.

The [anonymous review archive](output/ids_xai_anonymous_review.zip) contains the six-page manuscript, editable LaTeX, protocols, and aggregate evidence. It excludes raw data, trained models, and author identities. Supplement upload depends on venue policy. The authored report remains a separate ten-page document. All 47 unit tests, local manuscript checks and the original artifact verifier passed; none substitutes for author approval, required venue screening or peer review.

The impact revision reports a shared failure: on GeNIS scenario 6, all three models made zero false alarms among 1,266 benign records, while RF, XGBoost and the depth-five tree missed 3,643, 3,854 and 3,859 of 4,563 attacks. The six-page conference draft adds a contrast with [Almohammadi et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1989148), whose simulated healthcare federation reports weak LIME local fit and missed spoofing attacks. The two studies use different tasks and protocols; their scores are not directly compared.
